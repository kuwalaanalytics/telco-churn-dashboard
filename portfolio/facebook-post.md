## Version 1: The question every owner should ask

Do you know how many customers your business lost last month?

Most owners know how many new customers they gained. Very few know how many quietly stopped coming back, or how much money left with them.

At Kuwala Analytics we built an example to show what your own records can tell you. We used a public sample of 7,043 telecom customers (a practice dataset, not a real client) and found:

👉 1 in every 4 customers left in one month
👉 They took 30% of the monthly income with them
👉 More than half of brand new customers left within their first 6 months
👉 Customers on long contracts almost never left. Customers paying month to month left 15 times more often

The good news: the records also showed who was likely to leave next. By looking at just the riskiest 20% of customers, we could find half of everyone who was about to go. That means your team knows exactly who to call first.

We put it all into one simple dashboard you can open on your phone or laptop.

## Version 2: Keeping a customer is cheaper than finding a new one

It costs far less to keep a customer than to find a new one. But you can only keep them if you know they are about to leave.

We wanted to show business owners in Malawi how this works, so Kuwala Analytics built a free example dashboard using a public practice dataset of telecom customers. This is not real client data. It is there to show you what is possible.

Here is what the numbers showed:

✅ New customers are the most likely to leave. A simple welcome call and a check-in after 30 days can make a real difference.
✅ Customers who signed a 1 or 2 year agreement stayed. Customers paying month to month left much more often.
✅ Customers who had support services stayed far longer than those who did not.
✅ A short list of high-risk customers contained half of everyone who left.

With the right list, your staff do not have to guess. They call the right people, at the right time, with the right offer.
